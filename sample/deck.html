Peat & forest fire playbook · Kalimantan

Putting Out a Fire
That Burns Underground

Five suppression methods proven on peat and forest fires in Canada, Australia and Siberia — applied to Central Kalimantan.

Peat fire: heat, not flame.

In order of use: detect · surround · drown · re-wet · hold
Why Kalimantan is different

Surface water never reaches a 3 m smoulder

  • Peat is dead plant matter 1–5 m deep; it smoulders without flame.
  • A hose wets the top 10 cm — the heat stays below.
  • Drained canals drop the water table and dry the fuel.
2.6 M ha burned across Indonesia in 2015; the haze cost US$16.1 bn (World Bank).
Methods 01–02 · first six hours

Attack from the air, contain on the ground

01

Aerial water bombing

Helicopter buckets of 1,000–3,000 L and scoopers dropping 6,000 L per pass cool the flaming edge.

Proven: Alberta · Victoria · Riau, 2015 and 2019

02

Firebreaks and backburning

Excavators and hand crews cut 3–5 m mineral-soil breaks; a controlled backburn removes fuel ahead of the front.

Proven: US Forest Service · Australian state crews

Air drops buy hours. Only ground work kills a peat fire.

Methods 03–04 · killing the underground fire

Drown the smoulder, then keep the peat wet

03

Deep water injection

High-pressure pumps drive water through lances into the peat. Standard practice on Russian and Malaysian peat fires.

04

Canal blocking and rewetting

Drainage canals are dammed so the water table rises. Indonesia's restoration agency has built thousands of blocks.

Method 05 · and the rule that keeps it out

Find it in hours, hold the line with villagers

Find it fast

  • Hotspot alerts from NASA FIRMS and Copernicus, refreshed every few hours.
  • Thermal drones see smoulder under smoke and canopy.
  • Cloud seeding (Indonesia's TMC) adds rain before the peak.

Hold it locally

  • Manggala Agni crews pair with village brigades (MPA) on patrol.
  • Australia's volunteer fire-service model trains residents to hold the line.
